NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING (NLP) – INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

1. How Is Text Preprocessing Performed?

Text preprocessing is the process of cleaning and transforming raw text into a suitable format for Natural Language Processing (NLP) tasks.

Common steps include:

* Lowercasing: Convert text to lowercase to maintain consistency.
* Tokenization: Split text into individual words or sentences.
* Removing punctuation: Remove unnecessary punctuation marks.
* Removing stop words: Remove common words such as "is", "the", and "a" when they are not useful for the task.
* Stemming or lemmatization: Reduce words to their root or base forms.
* Handling special characters: Remove or normalize unwanted symbols and characters.
* Handling extra spaces: Remove unnecessary whitespace.

Example:

Original text: "The Cats are RUNNING quickly!"

After basic preprocessing: "cat run quickly"

Note: The exact steps depend on the NLP task. For example, stop-word removal and lowercasing may not be appropriate for every application.

2. What Is the Difference Between Structured and Unstructured Datasets?

Structured Dataset:

* Data is organized in a predefined format, usually rows and columns.
* It has a consistent schema and clearly defined fields.
* It is easy to query and analyze using SQL or tools such as Pandas.
* Examples: Student records, employee databases, and sales tables.

Unstructured Dataset:

* Data does not follow a fixed tabular schema.
* It may contain text, images, audio, or video.
* It often requires specialized processing to extract useful information.
* Examples: Emails, social media posts, photographs, audio recordings, and videos.

In short: Structured data follows a predefined organization, whereas unstructured data does not have a fixed tabular structure.

3. What Is POS Tagging?

POS stands for Part-of-Speech tagging.

POS tagging is an NLP technique that assigns a grammatical category to each word in a sentence, such as noun, verb, adjective, or adverb.

Example:

Sentence: "The quick fox jumps."

Possible tags:

* The – Determiner
* quick – Adjective
* fox – Noun
* jumps – Verb

Applications:

* Grammar analysis
* Text understanding
* Information extraction
* Machine translation
* Question answering

A word's POS tag depends on its role in the sentence. For example, "book" can be a noun in "Read the book" or a verb in "Book a ticket."

4. What Is Named Entity Recognition (NER)?

Named Entity Recognition (NER) is an NLP technique used to identify and classify named entities in text, such as people, organizations, locations, dates, and monetary values.

Example:

Sentence: "Riya joined Google in Kolkata in 2025."

Possible entities:

* Riya – Person
* Google – Organization
* Kolkata – Location
* 2025 – Date

Applications:

* Extracting information from documents
* Analyzing news articles
* Processing customer messages
* Identifying organizations and locations
* Building search engines and knowledge graphs

5. What Are Stemming and Lemmatization?

Stemming:
Stemming reduces a word to a shortened root-like form by removing prefixes or suffixes using simple rules. The resulting form may not be a valid dictionary word.

Examples:

* playing → play
* studies → studi (depending on the stemming algorithm)
* connected → connect

Lemmatization:
Lemmatization converts a word into its dictionary base form, called a lemma. It can use vocabulary, grammatical information, and context to determine the correct form.

Examples:

* running → run
* better → good (when the context and lemmatizer support this interpretation)
* studies → study

Both techniques help group related word forms, but their results and methods differ.

6. What Is the Difference Between Stemming and Lemmatization?

Stemming:

* Uses rule-based cutting of word endings or prefixes.
* Usually faster and computationally simpler.
* May produce a word that is not found in a dictionary.
* Often does not require grammatical context.
* Example: studies → studi (with some stemmers).

Lemmatization:

* Uses vocabulary and linguistic information to find a valid base form.
* Usually more computationally intensive.
* Generally produces a meaningful dictionary word.
* May consider a word's grammatical role and context.
* Example: studies → study; better → good in the appropriate context.

In short: Stemming cuts words down to root-like forms, while lemmatization identifies their meaningful dictionary base forms.
